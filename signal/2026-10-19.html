Who runs the operating layer.

A city ops brain for typhoons, a GBA diffusion-model bet, and free robotaxi rides in Tung Chung.

19 October 2026 · Weekly brief

All briefs

Opening note

This week was about who controls the systems we actually depend on. Hong Kong put a date on an AI brain for city emergencies; a Shenzhen team with HKU roots raised money to build language models a different way; and Baidu opened free robotaxi rides in Tung Chung — with a human still in the front seat.

Hong Kong · Civic AI / emergency ops

Hong Kong sets a timeline for an “AI City Brain”

Facts

On 12 Oct, Chief Secretary Eric Chan Kwok-ki told the South China Morning Post that Hong Kong’s “AI City Brain” could be tested as early as the next typhoon season, aiming to give authorities a “full picture” by connecting independent government databases. The data includes traffic, flood monitoring and drainage, landslide warnings, weather, and public inquiries under the 1823 hotline. Per the Policy Address path he restated: basic design in the first half of next year; first phase development completed and officially launched in Q1 2028. Dimsum Daily and The Standard the same day match that timeline and frame the system as an integrated early-warning and emergency-response platform.

GAIIN take

Hong Kong wants one screen that sees floods, traffic and landslides together — the hard part is getting departments to share data, not the AI demo. When the next typhoon hits, a human still has to decide whether to trust the system or overrule it. If you work with cities, insurers or infrastructure, those ground rules will land on your desk long before the launch party.

Question

If the city asked your organisation to feed data into this ops brain, what’s the one thing you’d refuse to share first?

Source: SCMP, 12 Oct 2026

Greater Bay Area (Shenzhen; team from University of Hong Kong) · Foundation models / alternative architectures

DiffuSpace raises for diffusion language models — from HKU into Shenzhen

Facts

DiffuSpace, founded in Shenzhen in May 2026 by HKU NLP Lab co-director Lingpeng Kong with HKU PhD researchers Gong Shansan and Ye Jiacheng and others, closed two consecutive financing rounds jointly led by Matrix Partners China, Shunwei Capital and Legend Capital, with follow-on from CAS Star, Huawei Hubble, Horizon Robotics and others. Star Market Daily, via sources close to the company, put the combined total at nearly RMB 500 million and called it the largest financing for a diffusion large-language-model (dLLM) startup to date; 36Kr described the total as hundreds of millions of RMB. DiffuSpace builds diffusion language models (parallel, iterative generation) rather than mainstream left-to-right autoregressive LLMs. The team’s earlier Dream 7B open model is cited with more than 2.5M Hugging Face downloads. Capital is earmarked for larger-scale training, infrastructure R&D and vertical adaptation; the company plans to release and open-source a new larger dLLM. Application focus in coverage includes code agents, end-side / embodied systems, and latency-sensitive use.

GAIIN take

Most chatbots write one word at a time; DiffuSpace is betting on models that draft and rewrite a whole answer in parallel — which can feel faster for coding tools and gadgets that can’t wait. The raise is nearly RMB 500m (per sources close to the company), but the thing to watch is whether the next open model actually feels snappier on ordinary hardware — and this team jumped the border from an HKU lab into Shenzhen’s chip and robot scene to build it.

Question

Where would a faster “rewrite-the-whole-answer” model actually help you more — coding tools, phones, robots, or somewhere else?

Source: Star Market Daily, 9 Oct 2026

Hong Kong · Physical AI / autonomous mobility

Baidu Apollo Go opens free Tung Chung robotaxi community rides

Facts

Baidu’s Apollo Go will run Hong Kong’s first community public autonomous-vehicle trial in Tung Chung from 21 Oct to 3 Nov 2026 (14 days), daily 2pm–6pm, for Islands District residents; Islands District Councillors get a preview afternoon on 20 Oct. The Transport Department backed the scheme in Islands District Council Traffic and Transport Committee Paper No. 36/2026 (13 Oct). The official annex caps the programme at 540 ride tickets (up to 1,080 people at two passengers per vehicle). Route: about 5.1 km / about 15 minutes from Yat Tung Estate Car Park No. 1 via Chung Yan Road, Yu Tung Road and Yi Tung Road to the Sheraton Hong Kong Tung Chung Hotel and back; no intermediate drop-offs. An onboard backup operator stays in the vehicle. Tickets are free, physical, single-use, and for the public only. On 10 Oct, TD’s Autonomous Vehicle Applications Promotion Working Group made its first on-site visit to the North Lantau / Tung Chung trial.

GAIIN take

This isn’t a closed airport demo — it’s a short public loop through Tung Chung, free tickets via the district council, and a human still in the car. That’s how Hong Kong is testing trust: let neighbours try it before anyone asks them to pay. If you work in transport, insurance or city tech, ask the simple questions — when the backup driver grabs the wheel, who’s responsible, and how does a two-week try become a real service?

Question

Would you put your kids, staff or customers in a free Tung Chung robotaxi this fortnight — and what’s the one thing you’d check first?

Source: Islands District Council annex, 13 Oct 2026

What to do with it

If a city, insurer or infrastructure partner asks you to feed data into this ops brain, write down the one thing you would refuse to share before the ground rules land. For models that rewrite a whole answer at once, watch whether the next open model feels snappier on ordinary hardware. For the Tung Chung robotaxi, ask who is responsible when the backup driver grabs the wheel, and what a two-week free trial would have to show before anyone is asked to pay.
