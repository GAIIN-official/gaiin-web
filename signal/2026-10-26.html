Europe

Who rebuilds the firm.

A Stockholm battery startup bets on AI plus real cells, Oslo rebuilds MEP engineering around agents, and property ops get a shared AI layer.

26 October 2026 · Weekly brief

All briefs

Opening note

This Europe week was about rebuilding how real work gets done, not another chat wrapper. A Stockholm team raised for AI that still has to test battery materials in a lab. An Oslo firm is building the engineering company itself around agents. And a Stockholm startup came out of stealth to put AI agents across the messy systems property managers already use.

Stockholm, Sweden · Deep tech · climate · AI for science

Holyvolt raises $45M to hunt cathode chemistries with AI and a physical lab

Facts

On 15 Oct 2026, Stockholm-based Holyvolt told Fortune it raised $45 million in equity to develop battery cathode chemistries outside the two chemistries China has scaled (NMC and LFP). Fortune lists investors as Zone II, CC.VC, and angel investors from defence and finance. A Fortune correction removed Fifth Wall from this round; Fifth Wall was a previous investor only. Valuation was reported at $245 million according to a person familiar with the company (reported by Fortune; not confirmed by the company). Holyvolt's materials lab is Wildcat Discovery Technologies (San Diego), which it acquired earlier in 2026; Fortune cites a The Next Web report of a $73 million merger figure that Holyvolt confirmed as accurate. Coverage frames the bet as pairing AI models with Wildcat's high-throughput physical testing (company and Fortune cite more than 225 projects, more than 85 customers including BMW, and more than 500,000 cells tested). First commercial push cited: DRX (disordered rocksalt), nickel- and cobalt-free cathodes, with commercialisation aimed at 2027 starting with unmanned aerial vehicles. Axios Pro (17 Oct) also reported the $45M raise amount.

GAIIN take

Most "AI for science" pitches stop at the model. Holyvolt is saying the model is only useful if you still build and test real cells, then feed the results back in. For anyone buying batteries, running fleets, or backing climate hardware, the useful question is not the demo. It is whether DRX (or the next chemistry) can leave the lab with a supply chain you can actually trust.

Question

If your organisation needed a new battery chemistry by 2028, would you bet on more compute, more lab cycles, or both? And who owns the failure if the model was wrong?

Source: Fortune, 15 Oct 2026

Oslo, Norway · Startup funding · enterprise AI adoption · construction / deep tech delivery

Oslo's Solvera raises $6.3M to be the engineering firm, not another tool

Facts

On 15 Oct 2026, Solvera announced a $6.3 million pre-seed round led by Seedcamp, with Construct Venture, Inception Fund, Momentum, Obos Ventures, PSV Tech, Sondo, The Nordic Web Ventures, and angels including Spacemaker founders Anders Kvåle and Håvard Haukeland (company press release). Founded in July 2026 by Arif Mirza, Pål Rasmussen and Thorvald Thorsnes, Solvera is building an AI-native building engineering firm that acts as engineering firm of record: clients pay for outcomes, not software licences or billable hours. Focus starts with mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP). Company says AI agents handle a growing share of design and coordination while building engineers keep judgement. Since launch it reports more than $1 million in contracted revenue in the first three months, a team of nine, and commercial projects in Norway including work with contractor Betonmast. Capital is earmarked to grow the Oslo engineering and technology team, open a London office, and accelerate the Solvera operating system.

GAIIN take

Our view: better design software alone has not changed how engineering firms get paid. Solvera is trying to change the firm and the invoice, not ship another plugin. That is a harder sell than a SaaS seat, and a clearer one for buyers who care about coordinated drawings and who signs the stamp.

Question

Would you rather buy AI tools for your current engineering vendor, or hire an AI-native firm that takes responsibility for the outcome?

Source: Solvera, 15 Oct 2026

Stockholm, Sweden · Enterprise AI adoption · AI implementation · proptech

Stockholm's Vessla exits stealth with $3.3M for AI agents in property ops

Facts

On 19 Oct 2026, Seedcamp announced it is leading a $3.3 million pre-seed for Vessla, a Stockholm startup building an agentic operating system for real estate. Participants include Inception Fund, Tiny VC, operators from Lovable and OpenAI, founders Lukas Saari (Tandem Health) and Fredrik Hjelm (Voi, Pit), and other angels. Tech.eu and EU-Startups match the $3.3M figure. Founded earlier in 2026, Vessla layers AI agents across systems property owners and managers already use (maintenance, records, suppliers, invoices), with humans retaining decisions that need judgement. Longer-term goal: connect leasing, building operations and investment planning across portfolios. Seedcamp and Tech.eu say Vessla is already working with some of the Nordics' largest commercial and residential property owners and operators (names not disclosed). Leadership named publicly: CEO Orman Gazvini, CTO Kasper Janehag, chief scientist Arash Dabiri.

GAIIN take

Property work fails in the gaps between systems, not in the absence of another dashboard. Vessla's pitch is agents that move work across tools you already pay for, while a person still owns the call that can upset a tenant or a lender. The thing to watch is not the demo. It is whether those Nordic landlords keep the agents after the first billing cycle goes wrong.

Question

In your own ops stack, which handoff between tools would you let an agent own tomorrow, and which one would you refuse to automate first?

Source: Seedcamp, 19 Oct 2026
