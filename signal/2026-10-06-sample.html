After the pilot, the questions get smaller.

Who owns the workflow, where the risk sits, and which room is worth the hour. A sample weekly brief — the shell later briefs will fill.

All briefs

A Signal brief is a short read: what moved, why it matters to someone who has to decide, and the sources underneath. This page is sample copy in that shape.

The short version

Most organisations are past the demo. The questions in a useful room are narrower than the keynote. Which workflow is worth a pilot. Who is accountable when the output is wrong. Which peer has already paid the switching cost.

That is the job of the brief. Not another tour of the model landscape. A note you can forward to the person who owns the work.

What moved

  • Tool use is widespread. Governance is not. The gap shows up as soon as a pilot touches customers, money, or a regulated workflow.
  • Chapters are comparing notes across finance, logistics and software, rather than waiting for a single playbook that fits every market.
  • The useful output of a room is often a name, a constraint, or a source. Rarely a new slogan.

From the room

Good judgement travels through trusted networks. A brief should carry the context a keynote leaves out.

On the Hong Kong & Greater Bay Area chapter page, the public figures already point at this split: day-to-day tool use on one side, incomplete governance on the other. The sample does not add a new survey. It shows where a brief would put the implication.

What to do with it

Pass it to the person who owns the workflow. If a sentence matters, it should survive the sources list below. If it cannot be sourced, it does not belong in the brief.

Next week’s issue replaces the open card on the archive. This file stays as the pattern: title, hero, body, sources.
