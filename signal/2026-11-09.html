Who lets AI make the call

When AI gets real authority over real work, who signs off?

9 November 2026 · Weekly brief

All briefs

This week, AI got closer to the controls. In Abu Dhabi, an energy giant is connecting AI agents across planning and trading. In the United States, a humanoid robot maker has listed on Nasdaq with about $465 million behind it. And in Beijing, a state planner said China will speed up a full AI law. For a global network like ours, the thread is simple. The tools are moving into the core of how firms run, and the rules are starting to follow.

ADNOC connects AI agents across an oil and gas business

ABU DHABI · ENTERPRISE AI · ENERGY

What happens when an AI agent's advice and an engineer's judgement disagree? At ADNOC, that question just got practical.

On 2 November, at the ADIPEC energy event in Abu Dhabi, ADNOC announced a multi-year partnership with OpenAI and G42. ADNOC says it is the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier, a platform for connecting and coordinating AI agents. Those are software that carries out tasks, not just answers questions. G42 signed through its subsidiary Inception42, and Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan witnessed the signing. It was one of four agreements that day between Abu Dhabi entities and OpenAI.

It starts with two uses. The first is field master planning, which pulls together data from thousands of wells, multiple reservoirs and hundreds of surface facilities. ADNOC says plans can be prepared in hours rather than months. The second uses connected agents to forecast crude supply, demand, prices and margins from production through to trading. The system will run on OpenAI's GPT models and G42's sovereign AI infrastructure in the UAE, and ADNOC says human experts keep oversight of consequential decisions.

ADNOC says this builds on ChatGPT Enterprise for more than 40,000 employees, with adoption above 97%, plus more than 200 AI applications and 70 robotics use cases deployed or in development. All of these are company figures.

GAIIN take. Most firms still run AI as separate tools. ADNOC says it wants one layer that links reservoir data, capital choices and trading, so those decisions are made together, not one after another. That is a plan and a promise. "Hours rather than months" is ADNOC's own line, and no results have been published. The part to watch is who signs off when an agent and an engineer disagree.

If AI agents linked your planning, your money and your sales, which decision would you still keep for a person?

Agility Robotics lists on Nasdaq with about $465M behind its humanoid robot

UNITED STATES · PHYSICAL AI · ROBOTICS

Humanoid robots now have a ticker. Agility Robotics began trading on Nasdaq on 5 November as AGLT, with warrants as AGLTW.

It got there through a merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI, a listed shell company (a SPAC). Churchill shareholders approved the deal on 2 November, with more than 84% of votes cast in favour, and it closed on 4 November. Agility's gross capital from the merger and related financing is about $465 million before costs. That is about $164 million from the SPAC's trust account, about $201 million from a share placement at $10 per share led by Foxconn, and $100 million already received (company framing).

Then comes the business itself. Agility says its newest humanoid, Digit 5, is on track for early-access deliveries in the first half of 2027, with general availability targeted for the end of 2027. It says it holds more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders for Digit 5, but adds that this is potential value subject to contract milestones and not current revenue. It is also expanding with GXO to two more facilities, and has an AGCO manufacturing pilot expected to begin in 2027. Those are company statements.

GAIIN take. This is a listing deal, not a standard funding round. The money matters because humanoid robots need factories, service teams and patient customers before they pay back. Agility's own release says the $300 million is not revenue yet. Now that the company is public, investors will be watching whether early-access robots in 2027 turn into repeat orders.

Would you trust a humanoid robot on your floor before it had years of public results, and what would convince you?

Source: Agility Robotics, 5 Nov 2026

China says it will speed up a full AI law

BEIJING · POLICY · REGULATION

China has said a full AI law is coming, but not what is in it.

On 3 November, at a press conference in Beijing, Wang Ruomeng, a deputy head of the National Development and Reform Commission's innovation and high-tech department, said China will speed up basic, comprehensive AI legislation during the 15th Five-Year Plan period. This comes from Xinhua, which is in Chinese only, so the translation is ours. Wang said China will build safety standards and ethical guidelines that cover the whole life cycle of an AI system. Xinhua Finance adds that laws will also cover shared elements such as data, computing power and algorithms, and key uses of AI.

She also said China will support "regulatory sandboxes" for AI in different fields. A sandbox is a controlled space where firms test products under supervision. Rules are to be reviewed on a regular schedule and changed when they stop fitting fast-moving technology. Wang added that China is moving the World AI Cooperation Organization toward full operation, with 38 countries as founding members.

The reports give no draft text and no date for a law.

GAIIN take. This is a direction, not a bill. Still, "whole life cycle" and "sandboxes" tell builders where the rules may go, from how data is gathered to how a model is used. For firms that work across borders, the practical work now is to track how Beijing, Brussels and others define the same terms.

If one AI law followed your product from training data to customer, which step would be hardest for your team to explain?
