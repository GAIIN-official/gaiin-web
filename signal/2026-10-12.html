Weekly brief

Who pays when AI fails, and who owns the stack.

Liability rules, a robot R&D hub in Causeway Bay, and a Copenhagen data layer.

12 October 2026 · Weekly brief

All briefs

Opening note

This week was about who carries risk and who owns the layer underneath. Hong Kong started naming AI liability; a mainland robotics player planted an R&D flag in Causeway Bay; and a Copenhagen team raised to become the data layer legal AI agents will run on.

Hong Kong · AI policy / regulation

Hong Kong will study who is liable when AI causes harm

Facts

On 8 Oct, during LegCo debate on Hong Kong’s first five-year plan and the Policy Address, Secretary for Justice Paul Lam said the Department of Justice will lead a dedicated task force on legal liability when AI products cause harm. Secretary for Innovation, Technology and Industry Sun Dong said the Digital Policy Office will create an AI commissioner role, and that commissioner will publish AI-agent safety-management guidelines with the Hong Kong AI Research and Development Institute. Sun also said the government will inject HK$1 billion more into the AI subsidy scheme, and that phase one of the Sandy Ridge data park could start operating as early as mid-2027.

GAIIN take

Liability is what turns a pilot into a contract. Firms that wait for the task force’s conclusions will still get asked the same questions by clients and insurers in the meantime. Write down now who signs off on agent output, what humans must review, and what you log: those notes become your negotiating position.

Question

If an AI agent in your workflow made a costly mistake tomorrow, who in your organisation would own it on paper?

Source: RTHK, 8 Oct 2026

Hong Kong / Greater Bay Area · Physical AI / robotics

AgiBot opens an international R&D HQ in Causeway Bay

Facts

Chinese humanoid robot maker AgiBot opened a Times Square office in Causeway Bay on 9 Oct as its international R&D headquarters, aimed at market partnerships, industrial applications, and university collaboration. Co-founder and co-president Jiang Qingsong and partner Zhu Jie attended with Hong Kong officials including Under Secretary for Innovation, Technology and Industry Lilian Cheong. The move follows AgiBot’s July start of Hong Kong listing preparations and a May plan for joint labs with local universities. Campus recruiting began at CUHK on 9 Oct, with HKUST and HKU sessions scheduled for 13 Oct. Jiang has said overseas business should account for more than 30% of sales this year.

GAIIN take

An R&D HQ and a listing path are not the same as paying customers in hotels and retail. The useful questions for operators are staffing ratios, uptime, content refresh cost, and who owns the failure when a service robot misbehaves in front of guests, which loops straight back to this week’s liability story.

Question

What would a robot vendor have to prove in a 90-day pilot before you would let it touch a customer-facing floor?

Source: The Yangtzeer, 10 Oct 2026

Nordics (Copenhagen) · Startup funding · enterprise AI adoption

Copenhagen’s Pandektes raises €13.5M to power AI legal research

Facts

Pandektes, founded in 2022 by Casper Laursen and Søren Brokær, raised €13.5 million Series A led by Munich’s Alstin Capital, with PROfounders, Scale Capital and existing investors. The company structures legislation, court decisions and administrative rulings and combines them with an organisation’s own data. It reports 500+ law firms, enterprises and public institutions across Denmark, Germany and Switzerland, sixfold revenue growth over 12 months, and about two-thirds of trial organisations converting to paid. Capital goes to European and North American expansion, team growth, and opening the stack via API.

GAIIN take

The durable legal-AI businesses will own the verified data layer, not the prompt UI. If you sell into regulated buyers, your moat is structured sources, audit trails and an API others trust: the same pattern that shows up in finance and healthcare.

Question

In your domain, would you rather own the interface your users see, or the data layer every agent has to call?

Source: EU-Startups, 5 Oct 2026

What to do with it

Write down who owns agent output in your organisation before a client or insurer asks. For any robot or agent pilot, list the proof you need in 90 days (uptime, staffing, failure ownership) and who signs it. If you sell into regulated buyers, ask whether you own the interface or the verified data layer every agent must call.
