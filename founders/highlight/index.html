From HKU to Shenzhen: a different bet on language models

Lingpeng Kong's DiffuSpace builds models that draft a whole answer at once, then refine it, for coding tools and on-device AI.

Most chatbots write one word at a time, left to right. DiffuSpace is betting on something else.

Lingpeng Kong is an assistant professor in computer science at the University of Hong Kong and co-director of the HKU NLP Lab. Before HKU, he was a research scientist at DeepMind. In May 2026 he co-founded DiffuSpace in Shenzhen with HKU PhD researchers Gong Shansan and Ye Jiacheng. The company builds diffusion language models: systems that sketch an entire answer in parallel, then rewrite it in a few passes.

Writing code is rarely a straight line. Developers sketch a structure, fill in details, and jump around to fix things. A model that can revise many places at once fits that habit better than one that can only move forward. The same idea helps on phones and robots that need answers without waiting on a distant server.

The team's earlier open model, Dream 7B, came out of work at HKU with Huawei Noah's Ark Lab. DiffuSpace later closed two financing rounds. The combined total is nearly RMB 500 million, per sources close to the company. Coverage names Matrix Partners China, Shunwei Capital and Legend Capital, with follow-on from CAS Star, Huawei Hubble and Horizon Robotics. The company says it plans to release a larger open-source model next.

Research that started in Hong Kong, a company built in Shenzhen, and an open question: can parallel drafting match today's models on quality at scale? DiffuSpace's next open release will be the first real test.
