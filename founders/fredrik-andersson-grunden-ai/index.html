A Swedish home for Europe's AI

Fredrik Andersson's Grunden AI runs AI models in Nordic data centres, so European companies can keep their data close and their costs steady.

For many European software teams, AI runs on an American cloud, at a price that can change overnight.

Fredrik Andersson wants to change that. He is founder and CEO of Grunden AI, based in Solna, just outside Stockholm. Grunden runs open models, the kind anyone can inspect and use, on its own machines in Nordic data centres.

Software companies can plug those models into their products without rewriting their code. Teams that simply want AI at work get Grunden Cowork, a shared workspace for chats and files that runs on the same Nordic machines.

The pitch is control. Grunden says customer data stays in Europe under EU law, prices are fixed for the length of the contract, and customers can move between models without rebuilding their product. For a company building AI into what it sells, steady costs matter as much as smart answers.

On 12 October, European software investor Monterro announced a majority investment in Grunden. Grunden will power a shared AI service for the companies Monterro backs, while still selling to everyone else.

Andersson knows the customer side. He has also led Medovia System, a veterinary software company, for years. Now he sells the plumbing other software founders build on.

The question for European builders: does AI that stays close to home make products easier to plan and price? Grunden's next year will help answer it.
